\section{The counterexample-machine thesis}

The dossiers support a narrow claim.  Generative models are useful not because a fluent transcript should be trusted, but because falsification often admits a compact terminal object.  Search can be heuristic; publication cannot.  The workflow that repeatedly succeeds is:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{formal statement}\longrightarrow\text{structured search}
\longrightarrow\text{small witness}\\
\longrightarrow\text{exactification}
\longrightarrow\text{independent certificate}.
\end{gathered}
\]

The strongest cases do more than negate a sentence.  They identify the first failing dimension, isolate the hypothesis that was insufficient, and leave behind a corrected boundary problem.  A mature theory of AI-assisted mathematical discovery should therefore evaluate systems by certificate yield: the rate at which exploratory computation is converted into exact, minimal, independently auditable mathematics.
