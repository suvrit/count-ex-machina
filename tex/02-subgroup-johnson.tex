\subsection{Subgroup Johnson stability}

For $H\le S_d$ and a Hermitian matrix $A=(a_{ij})$, define
\[
\eta_H(x;A)=\sum_{\sigma\in H}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
 p_{\operatorname{cyc}(\sigma)}(x)\prod_{i=1}^d a_{i,\sigma(i)},
\]
where $p_{\operatorname{cyc}(\sigma)}$ is the product of power sums indexed by the cycle lengths of $\sigma$.  The standalone problem was whether $\eta_H$ is real stable for every subgroup $H$ and every $A\succeq0$.

\begin{theorem}[\statusfalse: subgroup Johnson stability]\label{thm:subgroup}
The assertion fails minimally at $d=3$ for $H=C_3$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
t=4^{-1/3},\qquad
A_t=\begin{pmatrix}1&t&t\\t&1&t\\t&t&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are $1+2t,1-t,1-t$, so $A_t\succ0$.  Since $C_3$ contains the identity and two three-cycles,
\[
\eta_{C_3}(x;A_t)=p_1(x)^3+2t^3p_3(x)=p_1(x)^3+\frac12p_3(x).
\]
Take two variables, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$ and $z=e^{i\pi/6}$, and set $x_1=z$, $x_2=\omega z$.  Both have positive imaginary part.  Since $(1+\omega)^3=-1$ and $1+\omega^3=2$,
\[
(x_1+x_2)^3+\frac12(x_1^3+x_2^3)=z^3(-1+1)=0.
\]
Thus the polynomial is not stable.  Minimality follows because for $d\le2$ the only subgroup possibilities reduce to the identity case or the full $S_2$ Johnson polynomial, both stable.
\end{proof}
