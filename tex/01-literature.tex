\section{AI-assisted counterexamples}

Proving a universally quantified claim and refuting one are not symmetric
tasks. A proof must cover every instance; a refutation needs one witness, and
once that witness is written down the verification collapses to checking
finitely many hypotheses and a single strict inequality or collision. That
asymmetry is the operative advantage of counterexample search, and it explains
why the 2026 wave of AI-assisted mathematics has been so much more visible in
refutations than in proofs of comparable standing.

Three of them fix the scale. \citet{Zhou2026} refutes Connes's group-factor
rigidity conjecture --- that countable ICC groups with property~(T) are
determined up to isomorphism by their group von Neumann algebras --- by
exhibiting infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic, mutually commensurable,
finitely generated such groups with isomorphic group factors; the abstract
records that the result ``was obtained with assistance from GPT-5.6~Sol,
independently of and concurrently with work by OpenAI.'' \citet{DaiEtAl2026}
refutes Carlson's conjecture that the depth of a finite-group cohomology ring
is realized by the dimension of one of its associated primes, and the entire
witness is a single finite group: over $\overline{\mathbb F}_2$ the cohomology
ring of $G=\mathrm{SmallGroup}(128,859)$ has depth two while every
associated-prime quotient has dimension at least three.\footnote{The table row
this replaces said ``no associated prime has dimension two''; the wording here
follows the index, which is strictly stronger. Worth one look at the paper
before it ships.} There the AI role is division of labour rather than
authorship --- the TARS agent system located the group by autonomous search,
and the human author reconstructed the argument and rechecked the finite
computations independently.  \citet{ChenDeDiosEtAl2026} show that the
exponent~$2$ in Carbery's many-function almost-orthogonality inequality fails
for every $p>2$; Grok supplied the structural counterexample, and the
refutation was productive rather than merely negative, since locating the
failure identified the critical exponent and yielded the corrected inequality
for every integer $p\ge2$.

The same year produced at least ten more, spanning
\begin{enumerate*}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the Jacobian conjecture in dimension three, by an explicit Keller map
  $\C^3\to\C^3$ of constant Jacobian $-2$ \cite{TaoJacobian2026,Shaska2026};
\item the Erd\H{o}s unit-distance conjecture \cite{UnitDistanceRemarks2026};
\item the Gaussian completely monotone conjecture \cite{GuSellke2026};
\item log-convexity of Fisher information along heat flow \cite{ZouEtAl2026};
\item Litvak's Gaussian-minima conjecture \cite{Kunisky2026};
\item the pairwise-independent correlation gap \cite{RamachandraNatarajan2026};
\item near-quadratic Elekes--R\'onyai expansion \cite{Liu2026};
\item the Gaussian moments conjecture \cite{LongGaussian2026};
\item the $xz$- and Mathieu conjectures for $SU(2)$ \cite{LongSU22026}; and
\item Schur positivity of claw-free chromatic symmetric functions
  \cite{MatherneMorales2026}.
\end{enumerate*}
The list is illustrative, not a census. The census exists: \citet{AIMathIndex}
is a source-first index of claims that open problems were solved, disproved, or
advanced with AI, running to 424 entries across thirteen fields as of
4~August~2026, of which 89 are recorded as disproofs and a further five as
mixed outcomes.

That index is also the sharpest available statement of what this archive is
for. Its verification field is a label describing what somebody else did
elsewhere --- ``Lean checked,'' ``author checked,'' ``independent specialist
review: pending'' --- and it links to artifacts in third-party repositories
without holding or running them. To its credit it says so: Lean-checked means
the encoded proposition was accepted by Lean's kernel in the cited environment,
and statement fidelity, the comparison of that proposition with the intended
informal problem, is a separate question left open. Nothing there recomputes.
What follows is the opposite trade: many fewer statements, each of them
recomputed from source by \texttt{make verify} before it is admitted.

The examples above differ sharply in proof technology, but their certificate
profiles are uniform: a finite object is proposed, the defining hypotheses are
checked, and one strict inequality or collision is verified. That is precisely
the profile a build system can be made to re-execute, which is the observation
the rest of this paper acts on.
