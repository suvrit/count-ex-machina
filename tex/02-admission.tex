\section{Admission rule and audit standard}

A conversational archive contains many false statements that are mathematically irrelevant: temporary lemmas, deliberately strengthened targets, pointwise surrogates for integrated inequalities, and sufficient conditions invented only to test a route.  This paper excludes them.

\begin{definition}[Admissible archive counterexample]
A recovered disproof is admitted only when all three conditions hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The refuted statement is either published or publicly posed by another researcher, or is a standalone formal problem explicitly posed by the author.
\item The original hypotheses and quantifiers are preserved.  A counterexample to a strengthening is not represented as a counterexample to the underlying conjecture.
\item The witness is independently checkable from the manuscript and source package by exact arithmetic, a complete analytic calculation, or a rigorous interval certificate.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Certificate levels]
An \emph{exact certificate} reduces the contradiction to integer or rational arithmetic.  A \emph{computer-assisted proof} combines exact symbolic identities with outward-rounded interval arithmetic and an explicit tail estimate.  Floating-point evidence without such an enclosure is not admitted as a disproof.
\end{definition}

This filter leaves twelve formal disproofs in nine dossiers, one per section below.  The count is by formal statement: two Borcea--Br\"and\'en problems fall to a single Schur-positive stable polynomial, two more to a single positive-definite pencil, and a metric principle together with its convex-body specialization to a single Lorentzian cubic.
