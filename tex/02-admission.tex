\section{Summary of the counterexamples and resolution}
Some simple criteria emerged from the counterexamples collected in this report, and going forward, we hope to use these criteria (and potential refinements to them) as a yardstick for deciding which disproofs get added to the collection.
In particular, a refutation of a conjecture or the (negative) resolution of a problem added to our collection must satisfy the following:
\begin{definition}[Admission criteria]
\begin{enumerate}
\item The refuted statement appeared publicly before the counterexample was sought, be it in a published paper, a preprint, a problem list, a talk, or a public forum post. Importantly, we do not use the prominence of the statement or the standing of whoever posed it as a criterion for admission; the public appearance condition is purposely to exclude statements invented in order to be refuted.
\item The original hypotheses and quantifiers are fully preserved.  Counterexample to a strengthening or any modification of the original problem are not admitted as refutations.\footnote{It may well be that a modified statement is the more ``natural'' one, but given the automation involved, to be pedantically correct, we cannot admit any changes, unless the modified version itself satisfies condition~1.}
\item The refutation is independently checkable from the manuscript, through source code that implements exact arithmetic, an interval certificate, an analytic calculation, proof assistant, etc.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Most of the statements studied in this manuscript involve problems that are likely well-known to specialists but definitely not that well-known outside specialist circles. %There exist several curated catalogs online for ``famous'' mathematical conjectures; our collection does not seek to compete with them but to rather complement them by also providing a home to mathematically interesting.
Our collection is open to everybody for contributions, as long as the contributions meet the minimalistic criteria listed above. Moreover, we neither enforce nor advocate any particular choice of verifier, and leave that as a responsibility of the contributor. We include AI-generated analytic proofs, our own proofs, as well as source code based explicit verifications. All the material is accompanied by a git repo: \href{https://github.com/suvrit/count-ex-machina}{\fontfamily{ppl}\selectfont\itshape https://github.com/suvrit/count-ex-machina}, which is a living repo and will be the definitive resource on any bugfixes, improvements, additions or otherwise, and we welcome contributions. %: a short exact-rational program works, as does a development in a proof assistant (welcome but not a precondition), or another legit certificate. For the 17 counterexamples presented in this manuscript, we present verifiable certificates in the accompanying git repo.
