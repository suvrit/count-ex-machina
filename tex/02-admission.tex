\section{How we selected the counterexamples and resolutions}
A conversational archive contains many false statements that are mathematically irrelevant: temporary lemmas, deliberately strengthened targets, pointwise surrogates for integrated inequalities, and sufficient conditions invented only to test a route.  This paper excludes them.

\begin{definition}[Admission criteria]
A refutation to a conjecture or the (negative) resolution of a problem is admitted into our collection under the following three conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The refuted statement appeared publicly before the counterexample was sought---in a published paper, a preprint, a problem list, a talk, or a public forum post. Neither the prominence of the statement, nor the standing of whoever posed it bears on admission; the condition exists only to exclude statements invented in order to be refuted.
\item The original hypotheses and quantifiers are fully preserved.  A counterexample to a strengthening or any modification of the problem is not admitted as a refutation to the underlying conjecture.\footnote{It may well be that a modified statement is the more ``natural'' one, but given the automation involved, to be pedantically correct, we cannot admit any changes, unless the modified version itself satisfies condition~1.}
\item The refutation witness is independently checkable from the manuscript, through source code that implements exact arithmetic, a complete analytic calculation, a rigorous interval certificate, theorem prover code, etc.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Most of the statements studied in this manuscript involve problems that are likely well-known to specialists, though some of them are certainly less-known. There exist several curated catalogs online for ``famous'' mathematical conjectures, and our collection does not seek to compete with them, but to rather complement them by also providing a home to more modest problems. Our archive is open to all, researchers, students and amateurs on the same terms. Moreover, we advocate no particular verification technology: a short exact-rational program works, as does a development in a proof assistant (welcome but not a precondition), or another legit certificate. For the 17 counterexamples presented in this manuscript, we present verifiable certificates in the accompanying git repo.
